01 The Driver · off track

WHY I RACE

Racing isn't just something I do on a screen. It's the sport I grew up loving, and this year I picked it over everything else.

01 The question

Talent isn't
the ticket.

What if the next Formula 1 champion is sitting in his bedroom right now, and nobody knows who he is?

I genuinely believe that. Not as a theory, not as a YouTube concept. Somewhere right now there's a driver with world-class talent: fast, consistent, with the instincts for racecraft, and nobody is coming for him because he can't afford it.

Motorsport is one of the only sports where talent alone won't get you to the top. A kid who is faster than his competition gets left behind, not because he lost a race, but because his family couldn't afford it. That's not a sport. That's a selection process for wealth.

And somewhere in that gap, between the driver who makes it and the driver who disappears, there are people like me.

02 The decision

Two paths,
one seat.

I walked away from 225 days of building a growing YouTube channel. Not because I failed, but because I couldn't commit to two paths at once.

For 225 days I worked on that channel day and night, then jumped into the rig with whatever was left. The burnout of constantly splitting my focus between content and sim racing made the choice clear.

So I stopped, and went all in on this: the rig, the kart track and the Road to Racing. One path instead of two.

03 Canadian Grand Prix · Montreal 2026

From admiring it
to chasing it.

At the 2026 Canadian Grand Prix I wasn't just a fan watching from outside anymore. I was walking the paddock: not the Formula 1 paddock, obviously, but the Formula 2 one. I got caps signed by Rafael Câmara and Dino Beganovic from the Ferrari Driver Academy and by Alba Larsen from Ferrari's F1 Academy team, and Sebastián Montoya signed for me in the Prema paddock.

Something shifted that weekend. It turned racing from something I admired into something I felt I had to chase. The garages, the cars, the drivers walking past with a pen in their hand and a session to get back to: everything I'd only ever seen through a monitor was right there.

04 Why the sim counts

It isn't just
gaming anymore.

  1. 01The physics are realThe technique you build over thousands of laps transfers: trail braking, tyre management, spatial awareness at 300 km/h.
  2. 02The data is realEvery lap is measured. You can see exactly where the time is, and work on it the same way real drivers do.
  3. 03The teams use itF1 teams train their drivers on simulators worth millions and build whole development programmes around virtual testing.

So why is the door still closed? Why does the path to real motorsport still mean starting in karting at five years old, with a family willing to spend hundreds of thousands, maybe millions, just to maybe get noticed?

05 The experiment

Not here to complain.
Here to test it.

This whole project is a real experiment, not a series, not a concept: a genuine, documented attempt to go from sim racing to real motorsport and see how far I can actually get. No shortcuts, no scripted storyline, just the question: can it be done?

I think about the kid who was faster than everyone in his karting class and then just stopped because the money ran out. How many drivers like that are out there right now on iRacing, grinding thousands of hours in the sim because it's the only access they have to the sport they love?

That's why I'm doing this in public: every step, every lesson, every failure. If I make it even part of the way, it proves that sim racing is a real pathway.

The next generation might come from a bedroom, a screen, a wheel and a pair of pedals.

I don't know how far this goes.
I'm going to find out.
